### TLT — the control case

*Figures as of 2026-10-04, 5-year evidence window.*

TLT is in this analysis because it is the **most oversold of the three and the
one I would not buy**. It is the test of whether the JPM argument is real
reasoning or just a dressed-up RSI rule.

RSI is 23.5 — far more extreme than JPM's 34.5 — and the 60-day z-score is
−2.73. On the indicator reading alone it is the strongest buy of the three.

**The conditional evidence refuses it.** Days with RSI below 35 (n=157) were
followed by **−0.37%** over the next 21 bars with a 46% hit rate. The z-score
condition (n=284) gives **−0.99%** and 42%. Both are worse than doing nothing.
Oversold on TLT has not been a signal; it has been a description of a security
that keeps falling.

The context explains why. Price is 9.3% below the 200-day and **49.8% off its
five-year high**, and the Sharpe ratio over the window is **−0.80**. There is
no drift to revert *to*. Mean-reversion logic assumes a level the price is
pulled back toward, and a persistent downtrend means that level keeps moving
away.

Worth noting how much the window matters here: over three years TLT is 23.5%
off its high with a −0.27 Sharpe; over five it is 49.8% off with −0.80. Both
describe the same security. The five-year figure is the honest one for this
analysis because it is the window the conditional table is computed over, and
quoting a drawdown from a different window than the evidence would be picking
the flattering number.

One more detail worth flagging: GARCH persistence is **0.998 with a half-life
of about 423 trading days** — effectively a unit root in variance. The model is
telling me it cannot identify a stable long-run volatility level to revert
toward, which is itself a warning that the series is not well described by the
stationary assumptions behind the other two analyses.

**Conclusion: avoid, and treat a long position as a sell.** Not because it is
expensive — it is cheap by every oscillator on the page — but because the
indicator that would justify buying it has a measurably negative track record
on this name.

**Why this matters for the other two.** The same RSI reading implies three
different actions across these names: buy JPM at 34.5, trim AMD at 70.1, avoid
TLT at 23.5. Nothing about the indicator value itself produces that ranking.
It comes from the Hurst exponent deciding whether reversion is the right model
at all, the conditional table deciding whether the rule has ever paid here, and
GARCH deciding whether the current regime resembles the history being averaged.
